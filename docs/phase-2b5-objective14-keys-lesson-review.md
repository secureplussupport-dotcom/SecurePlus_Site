[NIST SP 800-63B-4 §3.1.1.2](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/#passwordver) distinguishes salted password hashing, cost and separately protected verifier secrets. [RFC 9106 §§1,3,4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9106.html) and [RFC 7914 §§2,6](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7914.html) support memory-hard password derivation and configurable costs. These informational publications do not turn every algorithm mention or historical parameter into deployment approval. A unique salt frustrates reusable precomputation; work cost increases guessing effort. Neither is a guarantee that guessing or all collisions are impossible. Hold the categorical salt wording and unspecific bcrypt prevalence/constraint claim for a later editorial edit.

[Microsoft BitLocker planning](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/security/operating-system-security/data-protection/bitlocker/planning-guide) distinguishes protected at-rest keys from in-use memory and protector/configuration requirements. [FIPS 140-3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/140-3/final) concerns module requirements; a lesson's HSM name is not validation of a product or custody. [Apple's Secure Enclave overview](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/the-secure-enclave-sec59b0b31ff/web) supports the integrated subsystem distinction, but this direct reference is missing from the lesson links. [NIST SP 800-38F](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/38/f/final) qualifies wrapping as key confidentiality/integrity protection; its specific reference is also absent from the brief KEK row. No specific construction or system was executed or certified.

[NISTIR 8202 §§3.2,3.4.1,7.2–7.3,9](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2018/NIST.IR.8202.pdf) supports key possession, stolen-key risk, governance, external-input and historical-data limits. Ledger validity does not establish input truth or a real human's identity. Do not reuse that overview's simplified sign-as-encrypt wording as a generic definition of signatures.
