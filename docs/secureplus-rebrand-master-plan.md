# SecurePlus Rebrand Master Plan

**Status:** Approved strategic direction with a small number of messaging decisions still to validate  
**Date:** 1 October 2026  
**Audience:** Product, design, engineering, SEO, and growth agents working on SecurePlus

## 1. Purpose of this document

This is the source of truth for the SecurePlus rebrand and landing-page redesign. It records the founder's approved direction, resolves the questions raised during review, and translates the strategy into an implementation brief.

The rebrand should position SecurePlus as an **adaptive Security+ study system**. Its advantage is the connected learning loop across practice questions, PBQs, flashcards, Concept Deep Dives, performance signals, and Smart Review. The product should help a learner identify what they do not understand, learn it, practise it deliberately, and verify that the weakness has improved.

This positioning is credible only if the experience visibly completes that loop. A new slogan alone will not create a moat. The product, landing-page demonstration, onboarding, and retention flows must all reinforce the same promise.

## 2. Approved strategic direction

The following decisions are approved:

- Move the brand away from a generic “question bank that helps you pass with confidence” position.
- Keep **passing with confidence** as an emotional outcome and existing brand asset, but make personalised weak-area improvement the differentiator.
- Focus first on learners who are already studying but feel stuck, have inconsistent scores, repeat random question sets, or do not know what to study next.
- Present SecurePlus as one connected learning system rather than a list of MCQs, PBQs, flashcards, and lessons.
- Explain the product through a four-step loop: **Diagnose → Understand → Strengthen → Prove**.
- Replace the scrolling feature-card showcase on the guest landing page with this product loop.
- Put authentic SecurePlus interface visuals beside each step of the loop.
- Keep the interactive five-question sample drill on the right side of the hero.
- Use one strong hero CTA below the message.
- Keep the proven organic-search topics and pages that already attract Security+ traffic.
- Add a personalised-study SEO cluster without replacing or weakening the existing SY0-701 keyword footprint.
- Build advertising around the pain of random practice, stuck scores, and wasted review time.

### Page hierarchy decision

SecurePlus will have **one main homepage**, not separate homepages for each audience. The immediate product and design focus is the rebranded homepage at `/`.

Different pages may serve different entry intents without competing with the homepage:

- **Main homepage:** the concise brand promise, Daily Drill, and a visual summary of the adaptive learning loop.
- **Search landing pages:** focused pages that satisfy a specific Google query and then introduce the relevant SecurePlus journey.
- **Campaign landing pages:** created later only when a paid campaign needs close message match.
- **“Why We're Different” page:** an explanatory About page linked from the homepage. It expands the product thesis and is not treated as a search or advertising landing page.

The existing `/sy0-601-vs-sy0-701/` page is an essential search landing page. It must remain a distinct, maintained entry point for learners comparing the retired SY0-601 exam with the current SY0-701 exam. It is not an alternative homepage.

## 3. Target learner and job to be done

### Primary audience

The first audience is a Security+ learner who has already started studying and feels stuck. Typical signals include:

- Their practice score has plateaued or changes unpredictably.
- They know they answered questions incorrectly but do not know what to review next.
- They keep taking broad, random MCQ sets and repeat material they already know.
- They have notes, videos, flashcards, and practice tools, but no system connecting them.
- Their exam date is approaching and they want to allocate study time intelligently.

### Core job to be done

> When I practise Security+ questions and expose gaps, help me understand exactly what I am weak at, give me the right material and targeted practice, and show me whether I improved.

### Secondary audiences

- New learners who need a clear starting path.
- Returning learners moving from SY0-601 knowledge to SY0-701.
- Time-constrained learners who want efficient revision.

These groups still matter for SEO and product access, but the homepage message should be written for the stuck learner first. Trying to make the hero equally specific to every learner would dilute the positioning.

## 4. Positioning and messaging hierarchy

### Category

**Adaptive Security+ study system**

This phrase communicates more value than “practice platform” while remaining understandable. Use “personalised Security+ practice” in learner-facing copy where “adaptive” sounds too technical.

### Core promise

SecurePlus turns practice results into a focused next step, helping learners spend less time reviewing what they already know and more time repairing the concepts holding them back.

### Product mechanism

Every relevant answer creates a learning signal. SecurePlus uses those signals to surface weak topics, connect the learner to an appropriate Concept Deep Dive or flashcard set, create targeted practice, and bring the concept back through Smart Review.

Do not describe the system as AI-powered unless the actual recommendation system uses AI in a meaningful, demonstrable way. “Personalised” must refer to observed learner performance, not merely allowing users to select a category.

### Emotional outcome

The learner knows what to do next and can see that weak areas are improving. This is the foundation for genuine confidence.

## 5. Tagline decision

### Recommended hero structure

**Eyebrow:** Security+ practice, personalised to you.  
**Large headline:** Diagnose your weak spots. Follow your path. Pass with confidence.

**Supporting line:** SecurePlus turns every answer into targeted practice for the concepts you need most.  
**Primary CTA:** Diagnose my weak spots

### Why this structure works

The large headline needs to be memorable, not carry the entire product explanation. “Diagnose” communicates that SecurePlus interprets performance rather than merely serving questions. “Your path” introduces individualisation, while the eyebrow and supporting line explain how it works. The final phrase preserves the strongest part of the current brand message.

“Diagnose your weak spots” by itself could still make SecurePlus sound like a score report. Adding “Follow your path” signals that the product continues beyond diagnosis. The short supporting line then makes the personalised practice mechanism explicit.

### Alternative to test

**Diagnose your weak spots. Strengthen them. Pass with confidence.**

This version is punchier and communicates the closed loop more directly. It says less about individualisation, so the eyebrow “Security+ practice, personalised to you” becomes essential. Use this as the challenger in copy testing rather than mixing both headlines on the page.

### Copy constraint

Keep the hero short. The longer explanation of the problem, learning philosophy, and product model belongs on the About page and in the product-loop section. The hero should create recognition and drive one action.

## 6. Guest landing-page specification

### Hero

The desktop hero should retain its current two-column structure:

- **Left:** alignment badge, eyebrow, large headline, one supporting sentence, one CTA, and a short no-sign-up note where accurate.
- **Right:** the real five-question Daily Drill interaction.

Use only one primary CTA: **Diagnose my weak spots**. It should begin a useful diagnostic or full MCQ practice journey, not open a generic pricing or signup screen. The sample drill already provides the secondary interactive path, so a second hero button would split attention without adding a distinct learner job.

The CTA note should set an accurate expectation, for example: “Start free across all five domains · No sign-up required.” If the destination or guest entitlement changes, update this statement at the same time.

### Product loop section

Replace the current generic feature showcase with a clear narrative:

#### 1. Diagnose — See what is holding you back

**Message:** Complete realistic MCQs or PBQs. SecurePlus converts misses and patterns into specific weak-topic signals.  
**Real visual:** A completed answer or set result beside a weak-topic panel, using realistic demonstration data from the product. Show the path from the answer to the named topic.  
**Avoid:** A generic score ring with no explanation of what happens next.

#### 2. Understand — Learn the concept behind the mistake

**Message:** Open the matched Concept Deep Dive and understand why the answer works, why the distractors fail, and how the concept appears in an exam scenario.  
**Real visual:** A genuine Concept Deep Dive screen with a concise explanation, comparison, or applied example.  
**Avoid:** Treating explanations as a passive content library disconnected from the learner's miss.

#### 3. Strengthen — Practise the weak area deliberately

**Message:** SecurePlus builds a focused mix of questions, flashcards, and review actions around the concepts that need attention.  
**Real visual:** Smart Review or targeted-practice queue showing the weak topic and the selected next actions.  
**Avoid:** Calling a static category filter personalised.

#### 4. Prove — Confirm the improvement lasts

**Message:** Revisit the concept through fresh scenarios and spaced checks, then track whether it has moved from a weak signal toward mastery.

**Real visual:** A follow-up result, review completion, or before-and-after topic state from the real interface.  
**Avoid:** Implying mastery from a single correct answer.

### Visual and interaction direction

- Use actual SecurePlus components or faithful captures of actual states. Do not use stock illustrations or invent product capabilities.
- On desktop, use alternating text-and-visual rows or a restrained sticky narrative in which the active visual changes as the learner scrolls.
- On mobile, stack each step with its visual directly after its copy. Preserve reading order and avoid horizontal carousels for the core story.
- Use one connecting visual motif, such as a path or progress line, to show that data from one step drives the next.
- Keep motion purposeful: reveal the answer signal, transition it into a review recommendation, and show the later retest. Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Use realistic demo data that cannot be mistaken for the visitor's own results.
- Each visual must remain legible at common laptop and mobile widths; decorative screenshots with unreadable text do not demonstrate value.

### Closing section

After the loop, explain the value of a free account: saving progress, preserving weak-topic history, and building a continuing Smart Review queue. Keep paid features clearly optional. The page should retain a genuine guest practice path so new learners can experience value before signup.

## 7. “Why We're Different” About page

Create a dedicated page that is accessible from the homepage and primary navigation or another consistently visible navigation location. Its user-facing label should clearly communicate **“Why We're Different”** even if the final URL is `/about/` or `/why-secureplus/`.

This is a supporting brand and product-explanation page, not a landing page. Its job is to expand the ideas introduced briefly on the homepage without making the homepage long or repetitive.

Move the longer brand story to this page. It should cover:

1. The learner problem: more random questions do not automatically produce better study decisions.
2. The SecurePlus belief: every mistake can become a useful signal when practice, teaching, and review are connected.
3. The four-step learning loop and why each stage exists.
4. Who SecurePlus is designed for, especially learners who feel stuck.
5. How personalisation works in plain language, including its current limits.
6. The product's commitment to CompTIA SY0-701 scope, accurate explanations, and deliberate review.

It should also compare the SecurePlus learning model with common alternatives in a fair, factual way:

- Random question banks provide volume; SecurePlus connects performance signals to a next study action.
- Standalone notes or videos teach concepts; SecurePlus connects concept learning to the mistake that triggered it and to later retesting.
- Static flashcards support recall; SecurePlus can prioritise review around observed weak areas.
- A score reports an outcome; SecurePlus should help the learner decide what to do next.

Do not claim that competitors lack capabilities without evidence or name competitors merely to create contrast. Demonstrate the difference through the SecurePlus workflow and real interface states.

Avoid a founder-centric company biography unless it adds trust. The page's primary job is to deepen the product thesis introduced on the homepage. The homepage should link to it near the summarised product loop with copy such as **“See why SecurePlus is different.”** It should also remain discoverable through the site's navigation or footer.

Every new or rewritten page must have one documented primary search intent before copy is produced. ## 9. Advertising direction

The following approved message angles should be retained:

- “Still doing random Security+ questions?”
- “Your practice score tells you what you missed. SecurePlus tells you what to study next.”
- “Spend less time reviewing what you already know.”
- “Turn every wrong answer into a targeted study plan.”

Organise campaigns by the learner's problem:

1. **Pain:** random practice and a stuck score.
2. **Insight:** knowing what to study next is more useful than collecting another score.
3. **Mechanism:** each answer helps build targeted review.

Measure the full acquisition path rather than optimising click-through rate alone: ad click → diagnostic start → diagnostic completion → free signup → first Smart Review action → retained review use. CAC is only meaningful beside activation, retention, and eventual LTV.

## 11. Product and trust requirements

These requirements are part of the brand promise:

- Personalisation must use observed signals such as missed questions, topic patterns, prior attempts, or review timing.
- A new user needs a clear cold-start path, preferably a short diagnostic or enough initial practice to create meaningful recommendations.
- Tell the learner why an item is recommended, for example: “You missed two questions on certificate revocation.”
- Let learners correct or dismiss a weak signal when it is not useful.
- Retesting should use fresh or meaningfully varied questions where possible.
- Never label a topic mastered after one correct response.
- Keep guest practice useful; explain when signup becomes valuable for persistence and longitudinal personalisation.
- Keep free-account value distinct from optional paid capabilities.

### MCQ practice-page direction

For signed-in learners, the MCQ page should lead with one obvious job: start the next personalised question set. Remove or collapse competing cards, promotional panels, tours, and secondary actions that distract from that decision. This requirement refers to surrounding interface distractions; it does **not** mean removing the plausible incorrect answer options within an MCQ.

The primary setup should contain:

- A concise **Personalised MCQ set** heading.
- One or two sentences explaining why the set is being recommended.
- Brief, decision-useful metrics such as due concepts, concepts needing attention, current chapter coverage, or the previous targeted-set result.
- A question-count control allowing a minimum of 10 and a maximum of 30 questions. Default to 10 unless product evidence supports another default.
- One dominant CTA, such as **Start my personalised set**.

Keep individual controls such as chapter, topic, difficulty, review mode, or other manual filters inside a collapsed **Customise practice** section below the primary action. Opening that section must not change the 30-question maximum. Do not allow the custom controls to visually compete with the personalised default.

The question-count control may use clear presets or an accessible bounded stepper, but it must never accept fewer than 10 or more than 30 questions. Preserve keyboard use, visible labels, and validation. When too few eligible questions exist, explain the reduced set rather than silently filling it with unrelated content.

### Free PBQ access decision

The baseline free-account PBQ allowlist must contain exactly these question IDs:

- `p035`
- `p033`
- `p223`
- `p094`
- `p017`

Remove all previously designated free PBQs from the baseline free allowlist, including any legacy single-preview constant or UI exception. A PBQ unlocked separately through an earned-access mechanism may remain accessible under that separate entitlement, but it must not be presented as part of the baseline free set.

Use one shared entitlement source for the PBQ bank, direct links, recommendations, guest previews where applicable, and PBQ launch checks. Do not maintain different hard-coded free lists in separate components. Verify that every listed ID exists in the published PBQ bank and that no retired free ID remains reachable through a stale client-side check.

### Free Concept Deep Dive access decision

Free signed-in learners receive access to **all Domain 1 Concept Deep Dive lessons**. Remove Domain 2 lessons from free access. Unless another explicit entitlement applies, Domain 2 through Domain 5 Deep Dives require Pro.

Smart Review may still recommend a locked Domain 2–5 lesson. In that case, show the lesson title, matched concept, reason for the recommendation, tier status, and upgrade path before navigation. The learner must still receive the underlying MCQ answer explanation and weak-concept diagnosis.

Implement Deep Dive access from canonical lesson-domain metadata and a central entitlement rule. Do not create a manually maintained list that can drift when lessons are added or renamed. New Domain 1 lessons should inherit free access automatically; new Domain 2 lessons must not.

### Exam Mode PBQ cap

Exam Mode must cap PBQs at **30% of the total questions by question count**. Learners may select a lower PBQ share, including zero where the experience permits, but never a higher share.

Calculate the maximum as `floor(totalQuestionCount × 0.30)` so the generated exam cannot exceed the cap. Recalculate and constrain the PBQ count whenever the total question count changes. The UI label, preview, generated exam, analytics, and final report must all use the same validated composition. Server or trusted generation logic should revalidate the cap rather than relying only on a slider or client control.

## 12. Smart Review and personalisation engine

### Product decision

Smart Review will be an explainable, concept-level mastery system. It will use patterns across **concepts tested, topics, chapters, question history, and time** rather than treating one wrong answer as conclusive proof that a learner is weak across a broad area.

One wrong answer must still trigger an immediate useful response: explain the miss, recommend the relevant learning content, update the learner's review material, and schedule the concept for another check. The distinction is that the miss creates a **learning signal**, while repeated or corroborating evidence establishes a weakness.

SecurePlus may describe this internally as a “mini AI” capability because it adapts to patterns. The public product should call it adaptive or personalised unless genuine AI inference is later introduced. A deterministic system with strong tagging and transparent rules is more trustworthy than an opaque AI claim.

### Current implementation baseline and gaps

The existing Smart Review implementation is a useful starting point. It currently considers direct misses, latest-answer correctness, wrong streaks, SM-2-style due dates, starred questions, topic and chapter accuracy, difficulty, time since last exposure, unseen coverage, and question diversity.

It does not yet support the proposed product promise completely:

- Questions usually have chapter, topic, and difficulty data, but do not consistently carry canonical IDs for every concept tested.
- Supabase attempt records currently persist question ID, type, title, domain, chapter, correctness, score, and time. They do not preserve concept evidence, selected answer, topic, response time, mapping version, or whether the question was an exact retry.
- Cloud attempt reconstruction therefore cannot reliably reproduce concept-level patterns from persisted history.
- The current weak-area flashcard builder mainly uses chapter, topic names, and text-token overlap. It does not use a curated relationship graph such as RTO → RPO → MTD → MTTR.
- Current chapter percentages are dominated by raw attempt accuracy. They do not distinguish limited evidence, immediate repetition, fresh scenario transfer, spaced retention, or sticky concepts.

These gaps must be treated as product work, not hidden through landing-page copy.

### Learning taxonomy and internal tagging

Use a stable hierarchy:

| Level | Example | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Exam domain | Security Operations | Blueprint-level reporting and coverage |
| Chapter/objective | Resilience and recovery | Navigation and aggregated competency |
| Topic | Business continuity metrics | Grouping related concepts |
| Concept | Recovery Time Objective | Primary unit of personalisation and mastery |
| Question | A scenario asking when service must be restored | Evidence about one or more concepts |

Every MCQ must be tagged internally with:

- A stable `primaryConceptId` for the main concept required to answer correctly.
- Zero or more `secondaryConceptIds` for concepts meaningfully tested by the scenario.
- The official SY0-701 objective code or codes.
- Domain, chapter, and topic IDs from controlled vocabularies.
- Difficulty and question format or skill type, such as definition, distinction, calculation, interpretation, sequence, or scenario application.
- A mapping version and editorial-review status.
- Optional misconception tags for distractors when a wrong option provides useful diagnostic evidence.

These tags are backend metadata and need not appear beside the question. The learner should see plain-language concept names only where they help explain a recommendation.

PBQs should eventually support step-level concept tagging because a learner may understand one part of a simulation and miss another. Until that exists, PBQ-level concept tags must not imply more diagnostic precision than the scoring supports.

Flashcards and Concept Deep Dives must use the same canonical concept IDs. Keyword matching may remain a fallback during migration, but it must not be the primary mapping mechanism after the concept graph is complete.

### Concept relationship graph

Create a curated graph that tells the system how concepts relate. Each relationship should have a type and editorial weight, for example:

- `commonly_confused_with`: RTO ↔ RPO.
- `prerequisite_for`: BIA → recovery objectives.
- `part_of`: RTO → business continuity metrics.
- `contrasts_with`: RTO ↔ MTTR.
- `applied_with`: RPO ↔ backup frequency.

A wrong RTO answer may add RPO and MTD material to the learner's review deck because those concepts are relevant. It must **not automatically lower the learner's RPO or MTD mastery score** without direct evidence. Related concepts influence content selection; only observed answers create positive or negative mastery evidence.

All automated or AI-assisted tags and relationships require human editorial review for SY0-701 relevance and accuracy before they affect learner recommendations.

### Learner concept state

Maintain one record per learner and concept. The implementation may use a numeric score internally, but the state must also retain confidence and review history. Recommended fields include:

- Current mastery estimate.
- Evidence confidence or effective sample size.
- Current learner-facing state.
- Number of distinct questions attempted.
- Recent correct and incorrect evidence.
- Consecutive misses and successful transfers.
- Sticky weight or lapse count.
- Last tested time and next review time.
- Last intervention, such as a Deep Dive or flashcard review.
- Mapping and algorithm versions used for calculation.

Recommended learner-facing states:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Not enough evidence | The learner has not answered enough relevant questions. Do not display a confident percentage. |
| Learning signal | One miss or mixed early evidence suggests the concept should be revisited. |
| Needs attention | A pattern across distinct questions indicates a likely weakness. |
| Sticky | The learner continues to miss the concept after explanation or targeted review. More varied evidence is required to recover. |
| Improving | Recent answers show progress, but retention has not yet been demonstrated. |
| Proven | The learner has succeeded on multiple distinct scenarios, including at least one delayed check. |
| Mastered | The learner retained the concept through a later spaced check. It remains eligible for occasional maintenance. |

### Evidence and scoring rules

The first implementation should be deterministic, versioned, and configurable. Do not bury thresholds in scattered interface code.

- A wrong answer creates strong negative evidence for the primary concept and smaller negative evidence for secondary concepts that were genuinely necessary to solve the question.
- A correct answer creates positive evidence weighted by difficulty, concept role, question quality, and independence from recent repeats.
- Correctly answering an exact question soon after seeing its explanation is weak evidence because the learner may remember the answer.
- Correctly answering a different scenario testing the same concept is stronger transfer evidence.
- A correct answer after a time delay is stronger retention evidence than an immediate retry.
- A concept cannot move to `Proven` while recent contradictory evidence remains unresolved. A later miss must delay or reverse promotion until the learner succeeds again on sufficiently varied and spaced evidence.
- Repeated misses after an intervention increase stickiness and the amount of future evidence required for `Proven` or `Mastered` status.
- A later lapse lowers mastery and schedules repair. Prior sticky history should make the concept recover more cautiously than a first-time miss.
- Response time may help with diagnostics only after accuracy is stable. Slow answers must not automatically count as poor understanding because reading speed and accessibility needs vary.
- Related-concept performance can influence which questions are recommended, but it must not fabricate direct mastery evidence for an untested concept.
- Chapter and topic competency should aggregate concept states and confidence. It must not be a simple average that lets a large number of easy questions hide an untested or persistently weak objective.

Initial thresholds should be treated as hypotheses to calibrate, not permanent educational truth. A reasonable version-one rule is:

- One miss: `Learning signal`; schedule a concept variant and the original question for future review.
- A second miss on the same concept across a distinct question: concept state becomes `Needs attention`. A cluster of misses across related concepts may separately mark the broader topic as needing attention, while each concept retains its own direct evidence state.
- A miss after a completed recommendation or repeated miss across later sets: `Sticky`.
- Two or more correct answers on distinct concept questions after repair: `Improving`.
- At least one additional correct answer after a meaningful delay, with no recent contradiction: `Proven`.
- A later spaced correct answer: `Mastered`.

Exact counts, delays, and weights must be configurable and tested against real learner behavior. They must not be presented publicly as scientifically validated until evidence supports that claim.

### Review scheduling and question-set composition

After a miss, schedule both concept transfer and question recall:

1. Put a different question testing the same primary concept into one of the next few eligible practice sets.
2. Make the original missed question eligible for a later retry, but avoid placing it immediately after the explanation when possible.
3. Add related concepts to the learning material and flashcard recommendation pool.
4. If the learner misses again, increase priority, vary the scenario, and shorten the next interval.
5. If the learner answers fresh and repeated questions correctly, lengthen the interval.
6. Continue occasional maintenance checks after `Proven`; reduce frequency further after `Mastered`.

Normal practice should remain balanced. Personalised remediation should not crowd out blueprint coverage or make every set feel repetitive. Use configurable caps for exact repeats, one concept's share of a set, difficult questions, and the total remediation share. A dedicated Smart Review session may contain a much higher proportion of due weak concepts than an ordinary mixed set.

The scheduler should consider both calendar time and set count because some learners complete multiple sets in one sitting while others return days later.

### RTO example: required end-to-end behavior

When a learner answers an RTO question incorrectly:

1. Save the attempt and its concept evidence for RTO, plus any legitimate secondary concept evidence.
2. Mark RTO as a `Learning signal`, or escalate it if prior RTO evidence already exists.
3. At the end of the set, show RTO in a prioritised summary explaining why it appeared.
4. Provide a direct link to the matched Concept Deep Dive. Show whether the lesson is available on the learner's tier before they click.
5. Tell the learner that RTO will return in subsequent practice.
6. Update the curated flashcard set with RTO and editorially related cards such as RPO, MTD, and MTTR. Label related cards as supporting review; do not label those concepts weak without evidence.
7. Schedule a different RTO/RPO distinction or application scenario in an upcoming set.
8. Make the original RTO question eligible for a later retry.
9. If the learner misses another RTO scenario or the original again, escalate RTO toward `Needs attention` or `Sticky` and increase varied practice.
10. If the learner correctly answers multiple fresh RTO scenarios and later answers a spaced check, move RTO through `Improving`, `Proven`, and eventually `Mastered`.
11. Reduce RTO frequency as mastery increases while keeping an occasional maintenance check.

### Post-set results UI

Every MCQ set result should include a prioritised learning plan, not only a score:

- Show the one to three most important concepts to work on.
- Explain the evidence in plain language, such as “You missed two different RTO scenarios” or “One miss; checking again soon.”
- Show the current state and avoid false precision when evidence is sparse.
- Link directly to the matched Concept Deep Dive.
- Link to the refreshed curated flashcard set.
- Offer a targeted Smart Review action.
- State when or how the concept will be tested again.
- Separate `weak` concepts from `related review` concepts so the learner understands why each item appears.

Core answer explanations must remain available regardless of tier. If a recommended Deep Dive is paid, show the lesson title, why it was recommended, its locked status, and the applicable upgrade path. Do not conceal the learner's diagnosis merely because the recommended intervention is paid.

### Signed-in learner homepage

The signed-in learner homepage is a study command centre, not the public landing page. Its first screen should provide clear, direct access to the learner's next best actions:

- **Continue your plan:** the highest-priority due or sticky concept with a concise reason.
- **Targeted MCQ practice:** a prebuilt set based on due concepts and current weaknesses.
- **Curated flashcards:** a dynamically updated deck containing weak and relevant supporting concepts.
- **Recommended Deep Dives:** flagged concepts with direct lesson links and clear tier access.
- **Resume recent work:** the most relevant unfinished or recently used activity.

Below these actions, show competency analysis:

- Per-chapter competency with an evidence-confidence indicator.
- Concept-level drill-down showing `Needs attention`, `Sticky`, `Improving`, `Proven`, or `Mastered` items.
- The evidence behind each flag and the next scheduled check.
- Recent movement, such as concepts improved, concepts that lapsed, and chapters with insufficient coverage.
- A clear distinction between mastery estimates and raw question accuracy.

Provide the requested per-chapter percentage once the learner has enough representative evidence. Pair it with an evidence-confidence or coverage indicator. Before the minimum coverage threshold, show “Not enough evidence” or label the percentage provisional. As coverage grows, chapter competency should become a weighted aggregation of concept mastery, objective coverage, question diversity, difficulty, and spaced retention rather than raw answer accuracy.

### Explainability and learner control

Smart Review must not remain a black box. Provide a short “Why am I seeing this?” explanation for every recommendation. The explanation should name the useful signals without exposing an incomprehensible score formula.

Allow the learner to dismiss or postpone a recommendation, report a bad concept mapping, and inspect recent evidence. Dismissal changes scheduling preference; it must not silently rewrite historical correctness.

### Data architecture requirements

The implementation should support:

- A canonical concept catalogue.
- Versioned question-to-concept mappings.
- Versioned concept relationships.
- Concept mappings for flashcards and Concept Deep Dives.
- Immutable or append-oriented attempt evidence sufficient to recompute mastery after algorithm changes.
- A per-user concept-state projection for fast homepage and review queries.
- Ownership-based Row Level Security for every exposed learner table.
- Server-validated writes or recomputation for any mastery state used for rewards, entitlements, or trusted reporting.

Do not rely only on a mutable mastery percentage. Preserve the underlying evidence so the model can be recalculated when tags or scoring rules improve.

### Implementation and validation sequence

1. Define and editorially review the concept taxonomy and relationship types.
2. Audit every MCQ for canonical primary and secondary concept tags; add PBQ step-level tags where scoring permits.
3. Map flashcards and Deep Dives to the same concepts.
4. Extend attempt persistence so concept evidence survives across devices and sessions.
5. Build a versioned mastery calculator and scheduler with automated scenario tests, including immediate exact-question recall, fresh-scenario transfer, delayed retention, repeated misses, sticky recovery, and post-mastery relapse.
6. Run the new system in shadow mode against historical attempts without changing learner sets.
7. Review false flags, repetition, concept coverage, and recommendation explanations.
8. Add the post-set learning plan and signed-in homepage surfaces.
9. Release gradually and monitor learning outcomes, engagement, and learner corrections.
10. Calibrate weights and intervals from evidence rather than intuition alone.

## 14. Strategic risks to resolve

1. **Positioning ahead of product:** If the recommendation loop is inconsistent or opaque, “personalised” will increase expectations and reduce trust. Audit the full loop before making it the dominant promise.
2. **Weak cold start:** A learner with no history cannot receive credible personalisation. The hero CTA and sample drill must lead toward enough signal collection without creating friction.
3. **Feature sprawl:** Showing every existing feature will recreate the old feature-list positioning. Each feature should appear only where it advances the four-step story.
4. **SEO dilution:** Rewriting high-ranking pages around the new brand can damage acquisition. Preserve primary search intent and measure before changing proven pages.
5. **Vanity-metric advertising:** High CTR from pain-led ads can hide poor activation. Optimise for completed diagnostics and useful review actions, not clicks alone.
6. **Unproven moat:** Integration is a promising differentiator, but it becomes defensible through accumulated learner signals, better recommendations, effective content mapping, and retention. A collection of linked screens is easy to copy.
7. **Bad tags create bad personalisation:** A sophisticated scoring formula cannot compensate for inconsistent concept mappings. Editorial tagging quality is foundational infrastructure.
8. **Memorisation masquerading as mastery:** Repeating the exact missed question too quickly can inflate scores. Require varied scenarios and delayed evidence.
9. **Black-box complexity:** More weights and states can make the system harder to debug without improving learning. Keep rules versioned, explainable, measurable, and removable.
10. **Paywall contradiction:** Diagnosing a weakness and then locking every useful action damages trust. Keep answer explanations and the diagnosis available; make tier limits clear before opening paid material.
