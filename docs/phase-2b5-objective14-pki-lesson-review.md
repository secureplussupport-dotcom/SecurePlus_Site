[RFC 5280 §§6.1–6.3](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc5280#section-6) distinguishes configured trust-anchor input, path constraints/time and CRL validation. A signed certificate asserts a public-key binding; its signature or self-signing alone does not establish relying-party trust. The lesson does not teach chain building, intermediate/root presentation, actual trust-store installation, hostname/SAN wildcard checks, time/purpose constraints or CSR proof of possession and issuance workflow. Missing teaching remains held; those facts are present in prior question/PBQ premises, not newly supplied by this title or definition. No certificate, CSR, chain or browser/server identity was validated here.

[RFC 6960 §§2.2,2.4–2.6,3.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6960.html) requires applicable signed status, authorized responder and freshness. A good result is not proof of issuance or complete certificate validity; unknown is not good. Cached responses and publication delay prevent instant awareness. The OCSP row supplies signature/scope/freshness direction; CRL and stapling rows omit those fuller prerequisites. Hold those compressed descriptions for explicit authority/applicability/freshness rather than treating a list absence or delivered response as full trust. [RFC 6066 §8](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6066.html#section-8) specifies client checking of delivered stapled responses; its historical message sequence is not generalized into every TLS version. No handshake, current status lookup or revocation recovery was executed. A direct stapling reference is missing from the supplied five links.

[RFC 8659 §§1,3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8659.html) makes CAA a compliant-CA pre-issuance authorization control, distinct from relying-party verification. It cannot guarantee prevention by a malicious/noncompliant issuer and is not browser-side revocation. The lesson's qualifier is retained; no CAA policy or DNS query was configured/executed.

[RFC 8551 §§2.4,3.1,4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8551.html) separates S/MIME signed and encrypted content and permits inner-header protection while outer metadata remains. Signing is not automatically encryption, truth or trusted human identity. Correct recipient keys, certificate validation, custody and sound endpoints remain premises. Hold transport wording: a TLS endpoint sees connection plaintext, which can still contain separately encrypted message ciphertext. No transport handshake, recipient encryption, certificate validation or endpoint assurance was performed; direct transport-protocol reference is absent.

[RFC 6376 §§1.2–1.5,3.4–3.5,8.2–8.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6376.html) separates signing domain from purported author and qualifies integrity to covered canonicalized headers/body; optional l= can limit covered body length.
