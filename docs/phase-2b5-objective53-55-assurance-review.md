## Twelve judgments and all wrong-choice corrections

**m080.** Identify investigation of vendor policies and assurance evidence as due diligence before an agreement.

HOLD a rigid due-diligence-before versus due-care-after boundary. Investigation and reasonable protective action can continue throughout a relationship. Report or certificate names alone do not establish security: actual scope, date, report type, exceptions, relevant customer responsibilities and remediation matter. No SOC 2 report or test result is supplied or inspected; no type or operating period is inferred. The question names an investigation, not vendor acceptance, risk transfer, completed monitoring or an approved supplier.

Main teaching judgment: Due diligence checks what is known about the supplier and its risks. Reviewing evidence helps a decision and should inform continuing oversight; possession of reports is not a security guarantee.

Wrong-choice contrasts, in current wrong-choice order:

1. Acceptance means choosing to retain a known risk; the described review gathers information before that decision.
2. Due care means taking reasonable protective action. The specific investigation in the stem is due diligence; these responsibilities do not occupy exclusive pre-contract and post-contract periods.
3. Transfer allocates specified consequences, often by contract or insurance. Reading evidence assesses risk and does not itself move liability.

**m091.** Identify the SLA function from measurable service commitments and agreed remedies; reuse the exact matching prior question judgment.

HOLD the name SLA as achieved performance, a recovery guarantee or unlimited compensation. Measurement windows, exclusions, response versus resolution and remedies require actual terms. Response time is not MTTR and an uptime target is not a per-outage RTO. The unchanged NDA correction is too categorical outside this naming example. MOU, MSA and NDA documents can contain or incorporate service commitments; document titles alone do not establish binding force or mutually exclusive legal functions. No numerical availability calculation is supplied.

Main teaching judgment: An SLA states measurable service targets and what the parties agreed should happen if they are missed. Recognising that function does not show the targets were achieved.

Wrong-choice contrasts, in current wrong-choice order:

1. The supplied emphasis is measurable service performance rather than shared intentions. A document called an MOU may still contain binding or incorporated terms.
2. Confidentiality is the NDA function used in this contrast. The retained statement does not rule out a combined agreement also containing service commitments.
3. An MSA supplies broader relationship terms and may incorporate an SLA; recognising the particular service-level function does not require separate documents.

**m100.** Identify a right-to-audit clause as permission for agreed examination of supplier controls and evidence.

HOLD audit rights as unrestricted access, an audit actually performed or verified compliance. Scope, notice, confidentiality, other tenants and applicable law constrain access. Without this clause, independent reports or legal rights may still provide assurance; reliance only on self-reporting is not universal. All three wrong corrections are unchanged and remain qualified: compensation and liability terms require the real contract, while force majeure applicability is contextual and can affect continuity/security operations. No actual enforceability, payout, liability cap or automatic excuse is determined.

Main teaching judgment: A right-to-audit clause lets the parties agree how evidence may be examined. Permission to inspect is separate from an inspection, its findings and follow-up.

Wrong-choice contrasts, in current wrong-choice order:

1. Indemnification concerns specified compensation obligations, rather than the audit-access function. Combined clauses and actual enforceability require the real contract.
2. A limitation clause concerns defined liability limits, rather than access to verify controls. It does not prove a particular cap or immunity.
3. Force majeure concerns contract treatment of specified disruptive circumstances. The retained natural-disaster description is not a universal exemption or proof that such events are unrelated to security and continuity.

**m190.** Recognise that outsourcing payroll processing does not automatically remove the organisation's applicable regulatory duties.

HOLD every vendor breach as automatically causing controller fines, payroll PII as sufficient to establish GDPR applicability, or an internal owner title as a legal controller role. The fine is a supplied scenario premise, not a legal conclusion or calculated sanction. Applicable jurisdiction, actual processing functions, duties and conduct determine responsibilities; processors may have their own duties. Contractual allocation or compensation does not simply cancel obligations owed to a regulator. Earlier controller/processor and risk-transfer holds remain exact.

Main teaching judgment: Hiring a processor does not automatically erase the duties of the organisation controlling the processing. Both parties can have responsibilities under the applicable law; the scenario does not determine a real case.

Wrong-choice contrasts, in current wrong-choice order:

1. A contract cannot simply cancel the parties' obligations under applicable law. Allocation of costs or remedies is a separate contractual issue.
2. A processor may have its own obligations; that does not automatically absolve a controller of its distinct duties.
3. Legal duties and conduct matter, rather than only the network where a breach occurred. The supplied fine does not establish automatic liability for every breach.
